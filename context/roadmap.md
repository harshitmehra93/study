   recall or mixed work.

4. Keep coding-mock, system-design, and behavioral practice moving alongside
   DSA, with emphasis on active timed work and concise senior-story packaging.

## Flexible Weekly Rhythm

Use this as a practical rhythm, not a quota:

- Make fresh mixed DSA the main practice on most weeks.
- Include one or two targeted recall or redo sessions based on current evidence.
- Add a coding mock or timed implementation when capacity permits.
- Keep at least one system-design or behavioral practice touchpoint; rotate the
  emphasis when the week is constrained.
- In a reduced week, preserve one fresh mixed attempt, one targeted repair, and
  one non-DSA practice block rather than trying to make up a fixed hour total.

## First-Ten Readiness Checkpoint

Review readiness after the first 10 fresh mixed attempts. Use the observed
evidence—independent model selection, correctness, implementation completion,
time management, assistance required, and repeated gap types—to choose the next
training mix.

The checkpoint should produce a baseline and concrete adjustments, not an
automatic graduation decision. Continue mixed practice, increase mock exposure,
or schedule a focused repair block according to what those attempts actually
show.
